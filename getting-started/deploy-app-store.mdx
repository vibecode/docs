**To publish an app on the App Store you need four things: a paid Apple Developer Program membership ($99/year), a signed build of your app uploaded to App Store Connect, a complete App Store listing (name, description, screenshots, privacy policy, and privacy details), and approval from Apple's App Review, which finishes 90% of submissions in under 48 hours.** With Vibecode you don't need a Mac or Xcode: open your project in the web app and click **Publish → Publish to App Store**. For native Swift iOS apps, Vibecode builds, signs, uploads, and submits the app for review; for React Native apps, it builds and uploads through Expo Launch, and you submit for review in App Store Connect.

*Last updated: October 10, 2026*

<Note>
  Publishing to the App Store is done from the Vibecode web app at [vibecodeapp.com](https://www.vibecodeapp.com), not from the Vibecode iPhone app.
</Note>

## The Publishing Process at a Glance

| Step | What happens | Who does it in Vibecode | How long |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Join the Apple Developer Program | Pay $99/year and verify your identity | You ([guide](/guides/apple-developer-account)) | Individuals pay while enrolling; Apple says to contact them if no confirmation arrives within 24 hours. Organizations first need a D-U-N-S Number (up to 5 business days). |
| 2. Accept agreements in App Store Connect | Free apps need the standard agreement; paid apps and in-app purchases also need the Paid Apps Agreement, tax, and banking | You | A few minutes |
| 3. Build and sign the app | Compile a release build and sign it with your Apple account | Vibecode | Expo builds take about 20 minutes, longer at busy times |
| 4. Upload to App Store Connect | Send the build to Apple and wait for Apple to process it | Vibecode | Apple's processing time varies |
| 5. Complete the listing | Name, description, keywords, screenshots, privacy policy, App Privacy details, age rating, price | Vibecode's agent helps; some fields are yours to confirm | Longest on your first app |
| 6. Submit for review | Send the version to App Review | Vibecode (iOS apps) or you in App Store Connect (Mobile apps) | One click |
| 7. App Review | Apple checks the app against the [App Review Guidelines](/guides/app-store-review-guidelines) | Apple | 50% within 24 hours, 90% within 48 hours ([details](/guides/app-store-review-time)) |
| 8. Release | Release manually, automatically on approval, or on a date | You | Your choice |

## Before You Start

You'll need the following before submitting your app:

1. **Apple Developer Program membership** ($99/year)
   - Enroll at [developer.apple.com/programs](https://developer.apple.com/programs/) or in the Apple Developer app. See [How to Create an Apple Developer Account](/guides/apple-developer-account).
   - Make sure you've completed payment and received the confirmation email
2. **App Store Connect access**
   - Sign in at [appstoreconnect.apple.com](https://appstoreconnect.apple.com/) and accept any pending agreements
   - Charging money? Also complete the **Paid Apps Agreement**, tax, and banking under **Business**
3. **A Vibecode plan with App Store submission**: Plus or higher ([compare plans](https://www.vibecodeapp.com/pricing))
4. **For Mobile app (React Native) projects:** a free [Expo account](https://expo.dev/signup). Vibecode uses Expo Launch to build and upload.
5. **For iOS app (Swift) projects:** your Apple ID, or an [App Store Connect API key](https://appstoreconnect.apple.com/access/integrations/api) with the **App Manager** or **Admin** role. Sign in with your Apple ID and Vibecode creates the key for you.
6. **An Apple device for 2FA:** keep your iPhone, iPad, or Mac nearby for two-factor authentication codes
7. **A privacy policy URL and a support URL.** Apple requires both for every app.

Your app is published under **your** Apple Developer account, so your name (or your company's legal name) appears as the seller on the App Store.

## iOS App or Mobile App: Which Steps to Follow

Vibecode projects are one of three types. Two of them can go to the App Store, and they publish differently:

| | iOS app (native Swift) | Mobile app (React Native) |
|---|---|---|
| **Code** | Swift and SwiftUI | React Native and Expo |
| **Runs on** | iPhone and iPad | iOS and Android |
| **Built by** | Vibecode, on cloud Macs | Expo Launch (EAS Build) |
| **Expo account** | Not needed | Required (free) |
| **Apple sign-in** | Apple ID or App Store Connect API key, connected once | Through Expo Launch |
| **Submits for review** | Vibecode | You, in App Store Connect |
| **Guide** | [Publish a native iOS app](#publish-a-native-ios-swift-app) | [Publish a Mobile app](#publish-a-mobile-react-native-app) |

Web apps don't go to the App Store. To turn a website into an iPhone app, see [How to Turn a Website Into an App](/getting-started/web-to-mobile).

## Publish a Native iOS (Swift) App

Native iOS projects don't use Expo. Vibecode builds, signs, and uploads the app to Apple itself.

<Frame caption="The Publish menu for a native iOS project">
  <img src="/images/screens/publish-menu-ios.png" alt="The Publish menu for a native iOS project, with Install and Publish to App Store" style={{ maxWidth: "500px" }} />
</Frame>

1. Open your project in the Vibecode web app, click **Publish**, and choose **Publish to App Store**
2. **Connect your Apple credentials** if you haven't already: sign in with your Apple ID (Vibecode creates the App Store Connect API key for you), or enter your API key's issuer ID, key ID, `.p8` file, and team ID
3. **Deploy your backend** if Vibecode asks. The published app uses your deployed backend.
4. **Confirm your bundle ID**, for example `com.yourname.habittracker`. You can edit it now, but it can't be changed after publishing.
5. Click **Confirm and publish**

Vibecode then shows its progress as it works: **Preparing your release**, **Uploading to Apple**, **Apple is processing your build**, **Updating your listing**, and **Submitting for review**. On your first publish, it may send you back to the project chat, where the agent finishes App Store setup such as your listing details and screenshots.

Want testers first? Ask the agent in chat to "upload this build to TestFlight," then follow the [TestFlight guide](/guides/testflight) to invite them. For the full native iOS workflow, including installing on your own iPhone, see [Native iOS Apps](/getting-started/native-ios-apps#publish-to-the-app-store).

## Publish a Mobile (React Native) App

Mobile app projects are built with Expo. Vibecode prepares the app, and Expo Launch builds it in the cloud and uploads it to App Store Connect.

## Run Preflight Before Submitting

Before uploading your app, we recommend running **Preflight**, an automated check that scans your app for common issues that cause Apple to reject it.

<Frame>
  <img src="/getting-started/preflight_button.png" alt="Preflight button in the deploy dropdown" />
</Frame>

**How to run Preflight:**

1. Open the **Deploy** dropdown for your project
2. Hover over the App Store submission section. The **Preflight** button slides in next to "Submit to App Store".
3. Click **Preflight**. This runs the `/appstore-preflight` skill in your chat.
4. The agent works through the checklist, fixes what it can, and reports what's left

**What Preflight checks:**

| Area | App Review Guideline | What the agent does |
|---|---|---|
| Privacy permissions | 5.1 | Removes permissions your app doesn't use and rewrites generic permission messages ("Allow VibeCode to…") into specific ones |
| In-app purchases | 3.1.1 | Checks the paywall is reachable, has **Restore Purchases**, and shows clear pricing; flags non-Apple payment code |
| App completeness | 2.1, 4.2 | Finds placeholder text, "coming soon" screens, and buttons that do nothing |
| Misleading design | 4.1, 4.3 | Removes fake user counts, ratings, testimonials, and countdown timers; checks labels match what buttons do |

You can also run the `/appstore-preflight` skill directly in the chat at any time.

<Tip>Running Preflight is optional but highly recommended, especially for your first submission. It can save you days of waiting for Apple to reject your build over a simple fix. See the full [App Store rejections checklist](/guides/app-store-review-guidelines).</Tip>

## Step-by-Step Submission Process

### Step 1: Start the Submission

{/*
**On Mobile App:**
1. Open your project in Vibecode
2. Tap the **app name** at the top
3. In the dropdown menu, select **Publish on App Store**
4. Tap **Get Started** on the welcome screen
*/}

**On the web app:**
1. Open your project in Vibecode
2. Click the **Deploy** button at the top right
3. Select **Deploy to App Store**
4. Click **Get Started** on the welcome screen

### Step 2: Verify Prerequisites

Check all four boxes to confirm:
- I have paid for the Apple Developer Program
- I have signed all required agreements in App Store Connect
- I have an Expo account
- I have my Apple Device ready to receive 2FA codes

If you haven't completed these steps, use the quick links provided to join the Apple Developer Program or access App Store Connect.

### Step 3: Add Your Expo Access Token

Vibecode uses Expo Launch to handle the App Store build flow. Before starting, add your Expo access token:

1. Click **Get access token from Expo**
2. Sign into your Expo account
3. Go to **Settings -> Access Tokens**
4. Create a new access token
5. Copy the token and paste it into Vibecode

<Tip>Use the paste button for quick entry of long tokens. Make sure your token is correct and there are no extra spaces or characters.</Tip>

### Step 4: Confirm App Details

Review and edit your app information:

- **App Name**: the name displayed on the App Store (2 to 30 characters)
- **Version Number**: starting version like `1.0.0`
- **Support iPad**: toggle on if you want your app to work on iPad. Apple then also requires iPad screenshots.

<Note>You don't usually need to update these details manually, because Vibecode can prefill them for you.</Note>

### Step 5: Confirm Your App Icon

Review your app icon. This is what people see on the App Store and on their home screen. You can generate an icon inside Vibecode.

### Step 6: Create a Bundle ID (First Build Only)

A bundle ID is a unique identifier for your app, like `com.vibecode.yourapp`.

**Important:** Once you submit your first build, treat your bundle ID as permanent.

- Vibecode automatically generates a suggested bundle ID
- You can customize it if needed
- Check the box to confirm you understand the bundle ID choice

<Tip>The bundle ID format is typically: `com.yourcompany.appname`</Tip>

### Step 7: Start Expo Launch

Once your app details are confirmed, Vibecode prepares the submission and asks you to start **Expo Launch**.

When you click **Start Expo Launch**:

1. Vibecode opens the Expo Launch flow in a new tab
2. Expo Launch handles the Apple authentication and build setup flow
3. Expo starts the cloud build for your app
4. The build is uploaded to App Store Connect

The build usually starts quickly. Full Expo builds typically take around 20 minutes and can take much longer during busy periods.

### Step 8: Monitor the Build

After Expo Launch starts successfully:

- Vibecode shows that the launch has started
- You can open the **Expo Dashboard** in a new tab
- You can close the Vibecode dialog once Expo has started the build
- The build continues in the cloud

### Step 9: Build Complete

Once your build is finished:

- Your app is uploaded to App Store Connect
- Your app is available in TestFlight for testing
- You can submit it for App Store review from App Store Connect (see [Complete Your Listing and Submit](#complete-your-listing-and-submit))

## Subsequent Builds

After your first successful build, the process is faster:

1. You won't need to create a new bundle ID
2. You can reuse the same Expo Launch flow
3. Start the launch again and monitor the new build from Expo and App Store Connect

## Complete Your Listing and Submit

Before Apple reviews your app, its App Store listing must be complete. For Mobile apps you fill this in at [App Store Connect](https://appstoreconnect.apple.com/) → **Apps** → your app. For iOS apps, the agent fills in what it can on your first publish and tells you what's left.

| Field | Required? | Limit or format |
|---|---|---|
| App name | Yes | 2 to 30 characters |
| Subtitle | No | Up to 30 characters |
| Description | Yes | Up to 4,000 characters, plain text |
| Keywords | Yes | Up to 100 bytes, comma-separated. Don't repeat your app name or use other apps' names. |
| Screenshots | Yes | 1 to 10 per device size, PNG or JPEG, no transparency. iPhone: 6.9" (1320 × 2868) or 6.5" (1284 × 2778). iPad 13" (2064 × 2752) if your app supports iPad. |
| Privacy policy URL | Yes | Must say what data you collect, how you use it, and how users can request deletion |
| Support URL | Yes | Must lead to real contact information |
| App Privacy details | Yes | Answer Apple's questions about the data your app collects (shown as the "privacy nutrition label") |
| Age rating | Yes | Answer the age rating questionnaire |
| Category | Yes | A primary category, plus an optional secondary one |
| Price | Yes | Free, or a price tier (paid apps need the Paid Apps Agreement) |
| Copyright | Yes | Year and owner, for example `2026 Jane Smith` |
| Sign-in information | If your app has a login | A working demo account for the reviewer that doesn't expire |

<Note>Apple has announced that from April 2027, apps built with the iOS 27.1 SDK or later also need iPhone Duo screenshots (1398 × 2034 outer display, 2007 × 2853 inner display).</Note>

Then submit:

1. In App Store Connect, open your app and the version you're preparing (for example **1.0 Prepare for Submission**)
2. Under **Build**, select the build Vibecode uploaded
3. Choose how to release it: manually, automatically after approval, or on a date
4. Click **Add for Review**, then submit it to App Review

Apple emails you when the status changes. You can also follow it in App Store Connect or the App Store Connect app.

## How Long Does App Store Review Take?

Apple reviews at least 50% of submissions in under 24 hours and 90% in under 48 hours. Your first submission and apps that need a demo account or special setup can take longer. If you have a critical bug fix or a time-sensitive event, you can request an expedited review. See [How Long Does App Store Review Take?](/guides/app-store-review-time)

## Why Apps Get Rejected

Over 40% of unresolved App Review issues fall under guideline **2.1 App Completeness**: crashes, placeholder content, broken links, and missing demo accounts. Other common reasons:

- **4.2 Minimum Functionality:** the app is basically a website in a wrapper, or doesn't do enough
- **5.1.1 Privacy:** missing privacy policy, vague permission messages, or no way to delete an account that users can create
- **2.3 Accurate Metadata:** screenshots or descriptions that don't match the app
- **3.1.1 In-App Purchase:** unlocking paid features with anything other than Apple's in-app purchase
- **4.3 Spam:** near-copies of existing apps or of your own apps

If Apple rejects your app, copy the message from App Store Connect into your project chat and ask the agent to fix it, then submit again. The full list is in the [App Store Review Guidelines checklist](/guides/app-store-review-guidelines).

## After Approval: Release and Updates

- **Release:** if you chose manual release, click **Release This Version** in App Store Connect. Your app appears on the App Store shortly after.
- **Updates:** make changes in Vibecode, then publish again. Each new version needs a higher version number (for example `1.0.1`), and every update goes through App Review.
- **TestFlight:** builds uploaded by Vibecode are available in TestFlight, so you can share a beta with testers before release.
- **Keep your membership active:** if your Apple Developer membership expires, your app is removed from sale (it keeps working for people who already downloaded it).

## Troubleshooting

**Build failed?**
- Verify your Expo token is valid and hasn't expired
- Make sure you've accepted all agreements in App Store Connect
- If Expo Launch opens but can't continue, complete any Apple authentication or team selection steps there
- Contact support via Intercom chat in the app if you need help

**Version number issues?**
- Each new build must have a higher version number than the previous one

**Bundle ID errors?**
- Make sure your bundle ID is unique and not already taken
- Follow the format `com.company.appname`
- Use only lowercase letters, numbers, hyphens, and periods

**In-app purchases not showing?** See the [RevenueCat troubleshooting](/monetization/revenuecat-setup#troubleshooting) section. The Paid Apps Agreement is the usual cause.
