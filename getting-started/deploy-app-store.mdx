**To publish an app on the App Store you need four things: a paid [Apple Developer Program membership ($99/year)](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/), a signed build of your app uploaded to App Store Connect, a complete App Store listing (name, description, screenshots, privacy policy, and privacy details), and approval from [Apple's App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/), which finishes 90% of submissions in under 48 hours.** With Vibecode you don't need a Mac or Xcode. For a native Swift **iOS app**, click **Publish → Publish to App Store**: Vibecode builds, signs, uploads, and submits the app for review. For a React Native **Mobile app**, click **Deploy → Submit to App Store**: Vibecode prepares the app, Expo Launch builds and uploads it, and you submit it for review in App Store Connect.

Still building the app itself? [How to make an app](https://www.vibecodeapp.com/blog/how-to-make-an-app) covers the steps before this one, from the first prompt to testing on your phone.

## The Publishing Process at a Glance

| Step | What happens | Who does it in Vibecode | How long |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Join the Apple Developer Program | Pay $99/year and verify your identity | You ([guide](/guides/apple-developer-account)) | Individuals pay while enrolling, and [Apple says](https://developer.apple.com/support/enrollment/) to contact them if no confirmation arrives within 24 hours. Organizations first need a [D-U-N-S Number](https://developer.apple.com/support/D-U-N-S/), which takes up to 5 business days, plus up to 2 for Apple to receive it. |
| 2. Accept agreements in App Store Connect | Free apps need the standard agreement; paid apps and in-app purchases also need the Paid Apps Agreement, tax, and banking | You | A few minutes |
| 3. Build and sign the app | Compile a release build and sign it with your Apple account | Vibecode | The agent's first cloud build took 5 minutes in our test, and the signed release build runs when Vibecode submits. Expo builds take about 20 minutes, longer at busy times. |
| 4. Upload to App Store Connect | Send the build to Apple and wait for Apple to process it | Vibecode | Under 5 minutes, with the release build and the submission, for a different app of ours on October 27. Apple's processing time varies. |
| 5. Complete the listing | Name, description, keywords, screenshots, privacy policy, App Privacy details, age rating, price | For iOS apps the agent writes it and asks you what it can't know | 6 minutes for the agent in our test; longer if you write it yourself |
| 6. Submit for review | Send the version to App Review | Vibecode (iOS apps) or you in App Store Connect (Mobile apps) | One click |
| 7. App Review | Apple checks the app against the [App Review Guidelines](/guides/app-store-review-guidelines) | Apple | 50% within 24 hours, 90% within 48 hours ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/), [details](/guides/app-store-review-time)) |
| 8. Release | Release manually, automatically on approval, or on a date | You | Your choice |

## Before You Start

You'll need the following before submitting your app:

1. **Apple Developer Program membership** ($99/year)
   - Enroll at [developer.apple.com/programs](https://developer.apple.com/programs/) or in the Apple Developer app. See [How to Create an Apple Developer Account](/guides/apple-developer-account).
   - Make sure you've completed payment and received the confirmation email
2. **App Store Connect access**
   - Sign in at [appstoreconnect.apple.com](https://appstoreconnect.apple.com/) and accept any pending agreements
   - Charging money? Also complete the **Paid Apps Agreement**, tax, and banking under **Business**
3. **A Vibecode plan with App Store submission**: Plus or higher ([compare plans](https://www.vibecodeapp.com/pricing))
4. **For Mobile app (React Native) projects:** a free [Expo account](https://expo.dev/signup). Vibecode uses Expo Launch to build and upload.
5. **For iOS app (Swift) projects:** an [App Store Connect API key](https://appstoreconnect.apple.com/access/integrations/api) with the **Admin** role, or **App Manager** with access to Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles, which Vibecode uses to upload and submit. Sign in with your Apple ID and Vibecode creates the key for you. On a new app's first release, Vibecode also needs a current Apple ID sign-in, because the key can't create the app in App Store Connect or confirm its App Privacy answers. If you connected with an API key, or your last sign-in has expired, the agent sends you a link.
6. **An Apple device for 2FA:** keep your iPhone, iPad, or Mac nearby for two-factor authentication codes
7. **A privacy policy URL and a support URL.** Apple requires both for every app. For an **iOS app (Swift)**, Vibecode writes and hosts both pages when you publish, adds them to your listing and links the privacy policy inside the app. For a **Mobile app**, have your own ready.

Your app is published under **your** Apple Developer account, so your name (or your company's legal name) appears as the seller on the App Store.

## iOS App or Mobile App: Which Steps to Follow

Vibecode projects are one of three types. Two of them can go to the App Store, and they publish differently:

| | iOS app (native Swift) | Mobile app (React Native) |
|---|---|---|
| **Code** | Swift and SwiftUI | React Native and Expo |
| **Runs on** | iPhone and iPad | iOS and Android |
| **Start from** | **Publish → Publish to App Store** | **Deploy → Submit to App Store** |
| **Built by** | Vibecode, on cloud Macs | Expo Launch (EAS Build) |
| **Expo account** | Not needed | Required (free) |
| **Apple sign-in** | App Store Connect API key, plus an Apple ID sign-in on a new app's first release | Through Expo Launch |
| **Submits for review** | Vibecode | You, in App Store Connect |
| **Steps** | [Publish a native iOS app](#publish-a-native-ios-swift-app) | [Publish a Mobile app](#publish-a-mobile-react-native-app) |

You can start either flow from the Vibecode web app or from the Vibecode iPhone app: tap **Publish** at the top of your project.

Web apps don't go to the App Store. To turn a website into an iPhone app, see [How to Turn a Website Into an App](/getting-started/web-to-mobile).

## Publish a Mobile (React Native) App

Mobile app projects are built with Expo. Vibecode prepares the app, Expo Launch builds it in the cloud and uploads it to App Store Connect, and you submit it for review.

### Run Preflight First

**Preflight** is an automated check that scans your app for common issues that cause Apple to reject it. It's optional, but it can save you days of waiting on a rejection over a simple fix.

<Frame>
  <img src="/getting-started/preflight_button.png" alt="Preflight button in the deploy dropdown" />
</Frame>

1. Open the **Deploy** menu (**Deployments** once your app is published)
2. Hover over the App Store section. The **Preflight** button slides in next to **Submit to App Store**.
3. Click **Preflight**. This runs the `/appstore-preflight` skill in your chat, and you can also run it there yourself at any time.
4. The agent works through the checklist, fixes what it can, and reports what's left

| Area | App Review Guideline | What the agent does |
|---|---|---|
| Privacy permissions | 5.1 | Removes permissions your app doesn't use and rewrites generic permission messages ("Allow VibeCode to…") into specific ones |
| In-app purchases | 3.1.1 | Checks the paywall is reachable, has **Restore Purchases**, and shows clear pricing; flags non-Apple payment code |
| App completeness | 2.1, 4.2 | Finds placeholder text, "coming soon" screens, and buttons that do nothing |
| Misleading design | 4.1, 4.3 | Removes fake user counts, ratings, testimonials, and countdown timers; checks labels match what buttons do |

### Submit With Expo Launch

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start the submission">
    In the web app, click **Deploy** (or **Deployments**) at the top right, choose **Submit to App Store**, and click **Get Started**. In the Vibecode iPhone app, tap **Publish** at the top of your project; the same steps open in a sheet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm the prerequisites">
    Check the four boxes: you've paid for the Apple Developer Program, signed the agreements in App Store Connect, have an Expo account, and have an Apple device ready for 2FA codes. Quick links help you finish any you haven't.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add your Expo access token">
    Click **Get access token from Expo**, sign in to Expo, go to **Settings → Access Tokens**, create a token, and paste it into Vibecode. Check for extra spaces when you paste.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm your app details and icon">
    Vibecode prefills the **App Name** (2 to 30 characters), the **Version Number** (such as `1.0.0`), and **Support iPad** (if on, Apple also requires iPad screenshots). Review your app icon too; you can generate one in Vibecode.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a bundle ID (first build only)">
    A bundle ID is your app's unique identifier, like `com.yourcompany.appname`. Vibecode suggests one and you can change it, but treat it as permanent once your first build is submitted.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start Expo Launch">
    Click **Start Expo Launch**. It opens in a new tab, handles Apple sign-in and build setup, starts the cloud build, and uploads the build to App Store Connect. Builds usually take about 20 minutes, longer at busy times. You can close the Vibecode dialog once the build has started and follow it in the **Expo Dashboard**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit for review">
    When the build finishes, it's in App Store Connect and available in TestFlight. [Complete your listing and submit it](#complete-your-listing-and-submit).
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Later Builds

You won't need a new bundle ID. Start Expo Launch again with a higher version number, then follow the new build in Expo and App Store Connect.

## Complete Your Listing and Submit

Before Apple reviews your app, its App Store listing must be complete. For Mobile apps you fill this in at [App Store Connect](https://appstoreconnect.apple.com/) → **Apps** → your app. For iOS apps, the agent fills in what it can on your first publish and tells you what's left.

| Field | Required? | Limit or format |
|---|---|---|
| App name | Yes | 2 to 30 characters |
| Subtitle | No | Up to 30 characters |
| Description | Yes | Up to 4,000 characters, plain text |
| Keywords | Yes | Up to 100 bytes, comma-separated. Don't repeat your app name or use other apps' names. |
| Screenshots | Yes | 1 to 10 per device size, PNG or JPEG, no transparency. For iPhone, Apple requires the 6.1" Dynamic Island size (1179 × 2556 or 1206 × 2622); add iPad 13" (2064 × 2752) if your app supports iPad. Other sizes: [Apple's screenshot specifications](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/screenshot-specifications/). |
| Privacy policy URL | Yes | Must say what data you collect, how you use it, and how users can request deletion |
| Support URL | Yes | Must lead to real contact information |
| App Privacy details | Yes | Answer Apple's questions about the data your app collects (shown as the "privacy nutrition label") |
| Age rating | Yes | Answer the age rating questionnaire |
| Category | Yes | A primary category, plus an optional secondary one |
| Price | Yes | Free, or a price tier (paid apps need the Paid Apps Agreement) |
| Copyright | Yes | Year and owner, for example `2026 Jane Smith` |
| Sign-in information | If your app has a login | A working demo account for the reviewer that doesn't expire |

<Note>Apple has announced that from April 2027, apps built with the iOS 27.1 SDK or later also need iPhone Duo screenshots, 1398 × 2034 for the outer display and 2007 × 2853 for the inner one ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/app-information/screenshot-specifications/)). Also from April 2027, iPhone apps uploaded to App Store Connect must be built with the iOS 27 SDK or later and target iOS 15 or later ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/submitting/)).</Note>

For Mobile apps, then submit in App Store Connect:

1. Open your app and the version you're preparing (for example **1.0 Prepare for Submission**)
2. Under **Build**, select the build Expo Launch uploaded
3. Choose how to release it: manually, automatically after approval, or on a date
4. Click **Add for Review**, then submit it to App Review

Apple emails you when the status changes. You can also follow it in App Store Connect or the App Store Connect app.

## Questions Apple Asks on Your First Submission

Besides the listing, Apple asks a few questions before it takes a new app. For iOS apps, the agent answers App Privacy, the age rating and encryption from your app's code. EU trader status is yours to set.

- **App Privacy.** What data the app collects and whether it tracks people. Your answers become the privacy label on your App Store page, and Apple needs them for every new app and update ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/)). Mochi keeps everything on the phone, so we answered **Data Not Collected**.
- **Age rating.** A questionnaire about your app's content and features. The ratings are 4+, 9+, 13+, 16+ and 18+, and AI chat features count toward the answers ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=ks775ehf)). It also asks about social media features, and Apple has required those answers for new apps and updates since September 2026 ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=tlur8uvi)). For iOS apps, the agent answers from your app's code. When nothing applies, it answers None or No to every question, which gives a 4+ rating.
- **Encryption (export compliance).** Whether the app uses encryption beyond what Apple's system provides, such as HTTPS. Vibecode's iOS apps answer No in the app's Info.plist file (`ITSAppUsesNonExemptEncryption`), which is right unless you add your own encryption ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/determine-and-upload-app-encryption-documentation/)).
- **EU trader status.** Under the EU's Digital Services Act, every developer says whether they're a trader, even if they don't sell in the EU. A trader's address, phone number and email appear on the app's App Store page in the EU. The Account Holder or an Admin sets this once for your account, in App Store Connect under **Business → Agreements** ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-compliance-information/manage-european-union-digital-services-act-trader-requirements/)).
- **Accessibility (optional).** You can list the accessibility features your app supports, like VoiceOver or larger text ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/submitting/)).

## After You Submit

- **Review:** see [How Long Does App Store Review Take?](/guides/app-store-review-time) for timelines and expedited reviews.
- **Rejections:** paste Apple's message from App Store Connect into your project chat, ask the agent to fix it, and submit again. The [App Store rejections checklist](/guides/app-store-review-guidelines) covers the common reasons.
- **Release:** if you chose manual release, click **Release This Version** in App Store Connect. Your app appears on the App Store shortly after.
- **Updates:** make changes in Vibecode, then publish again. Each new version needs a higher version number (for example `1.0.1`), and every update goes through App Review.
- **TestFlight:** builds that reach App Store Connect are available in [TestFlight](/guides/testflight), so you can share a beta before release.
- **Keep your membership active:** if your Apple Developer membership expires, your apps are no longer available for download, and you can't submit new apps or updates ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/support/renewal/)).

## Why Apps Get Rejected

Apple says that "over 40% of unresolved issues are related to guideline 2.1: App Completeness, which covers crashes, placeholder content, incomplete information, and more" ([App Review](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)). Apple's [list of common issues](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/#common-app-rejections) points to these guidelines:

| Guideline | What Apple checks | How to pass |
|---|---|---|
| [2.1 App Completeness](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#app-completeness) | Crashes, placeholder text, broken links, and a login the reviewer can't get past | Tap through every screen in the simulator and on your iPhone (**Publish → Install**), and give Apple a demo account if your app has sign-in |
| [5.1.1 Data Collection and Storage](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#data-collection-and-storage) | A privacy policy, clear permission messages, and a way to delete your account in apps that have accounts | Vibecode adds the privacy policy for iOS apps. If people sign up, ask the agent for a Delete account button. |
| [4.2 Minimum Functionality](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#minimum-functionality) | Apps that are a website in a wrapper, or do too little | Build real features into the app itself |
| [2.3 Accurate Metadata](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#accurate-metadata) | Screenshots and text that match what the app does | Use screenshots of the real app, not mockups |

For Mobile app projects, run [Preflight](#run-preflight-first) before you submit. For iOS apps, the agent runs a readiness check before it submits. If Apple rejects your app anyway, paste Apple's message into your project chat and ask the agent to fix it. The [rejections checklist](/guides/app-store-review-guidelines) covers more.

## Troubleshooting

**Asked to sign in with your Apple ID?**
- Vibecode needs a fresh Apple ID sign-in to create a new app in App Store Connect and answer Apple's privacy questions. Your App Store Connect key can't do those two steps, and Apple's sign-ins expire.
- Open the link the agent sends, sign in, and enter the code Apple sends to your devices. The agent picks the publish back up after you sign in. If it doesn't, send it a message.
- If your Apple ID belongs to more than one developer team, pick the team you publish with.
- Created the app yourself in App Store Connect? Tell the agent. On a first release it still needs your sign-in to confirm the App Privacy answers before it submits.

**Apple says you need to accept an agreement?**
- Until the Account Holder accepts Apple's latest agreements, Apple blocks your team's App Store Connect key, so Vibecode can't publish. The Account Holder accepts every pending agreement in [App Store Connect](https://appstoreconnect.apple.com/business) under **Business → Agreements**, and any banner at [developer.apple.com/account](https://developer.apple.com/account). If this came up while you connected your Apple account, sign in again on the Connect Apple Account page. If the agent asked during a publish, tell it once you've accepted, and it carries on.

**App name already taken?**
- Names are unique across the App Store, so a common name may be gone. When the agent creates the app, it adds a short suffix to a taken name and tells you the final name. If you create the app yourself, try a short description after a colon, such as "Mochi: Habit Tracker".

**Expo build failed?**
- Verify your Expo token is valid and hasn't expired
- Make sure you've accepted all agreements in App Store Connect
- If Expo Launch opens but can't continue, complete any Apple authentication or team selection steps there
- Contact support via Intercom chat in the app if you need help

**Version number issues?**
- Each new build must have a higher version number than the previous one

**Bundle ID errors?**
- Make sure your bundle ID is unique and not already taken
- Follow the format `com.company.appname`
- Use only lowercase letters, numbers, hyphens, and periods

**In-app purchases not showing?** See the [RevenueCat troubleshooting](/monetization/revenuecat-setup#troubleshooting) section. The Paid Apps Agreement is the usual cause.

## Which App Builders Submit to the App Store for You

Every route to the App Store needs Apple's Developer Program, at $99 a year ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/programs/enroll/)). What differs is who builds the app, who uploads it and who presses submit. We checked each tool's own docs on October 29, 2026:

## Frequently Asked Questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How much does it cost to put an app on the App Store?">
    $99 per year for the Apple Developer Program, which every App Store app needs. Apple takes 15% of sales if you're in the [App Store Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/) (up to $1 million in proceeds a year), or the standard commission above that. App Store submission in Vibecode is included from the Plus plan ($20/month), and builds use credits. See [what an Apple Developer account costs](/guides/apple-developer-account#how-much-does-an-apple-developer-account-cost).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="How long does it take to publish an app on the App Store?">
    In our test, the agent had done everything it could on its own 27 minutes after we pressed Send. A new app's first release then needs your Apple ID sign-in, and for a different app we submitted on October 27, the final build, upload and submission took under 5 minutes. Apple then reviews at least 50% of submissions within 24 hours and 90% within 48 hours ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/distribute/app-review/)). On your first app, also allow time for Apple to approve your developer account ([guide](/guides/apple-developer-account)).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I publish an app to the App Store for free?">
    No. Apple requires a paid Apple Developer Program membership ($99/year) to publish, even for free apps. Nonprofits, accredited schools, and government entities can request a [fee waiver](https://developer.apple.com/support/fee-waiver/). Your app itself can be free to download.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do I need a Mac to publish an iPhone app?">
    Not with Vibecode, which builds your app in the cloud. You only need an Apple device to receive two-factor authentication codes. See [How to Publish an iPhone App Without a Mac](/guides/publish-app-without-mac).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Do I need to sign in with my Apple ID to publish?">
    Yes, on a new app's first release. Vibecode uploads and submits with your App Store Connect key, but creating the app in App Store Connect and confirming its App Privacy answers need an Apple ID sign-in. The agent sends you a link when it needs one, and Apple sends a code to your iPhone or Mac. Updates to an approved app keep its App Privacy answers.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I choose when my app goes live?">
    Yes. Tell the agent which release you want: manual, automatic after Apple approves it, or on a date. With manual release, the approved app waits until you click **Release This Version** in App Store Connect, or ask the agent to release it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I publish from the Vibecode iPhone app?">
    Yes. Open your project and tap **Publish** at the top. iOS app projects offer **Install** and the App Store; Mobile app projects open the same App Store submission steps as the web app.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can I publish an app without knowing how to code?">
    Yes. You describe the app in Vibecode, the agent builds it, and the publish flow handles building, signing, and uploading. You'll still make the decisions Apple asks for, like your app's name, price, privacy answers, and age rating.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Whose name is on the App Store listing?">
    Yours. Vibecode publishes with your Apple Developer account, so the seller is your legal name (individual accounts) or your organization's legal name (organization accounts).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Can Vibecode publish to Google Play?">
    Vibecode's built-in publishing flow is for the Apple App Store. Mobile app (React Native) projects also run on Android: on the Pro and Max plans you can [download the code](/getting-started/download-code) and publish it on Google Play with your own Play Console account ($25 one-time).
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What happens if Apple rejects my app?">
    Apple tells you which guideline the app didn't meet in App Store Connect. Fix the issue (paste the message into your project chat and ask the agent), then submit again. If you think the reviewer misunderstood your app, you can reply in App Store Connect or appeal to the App Review Board.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
